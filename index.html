The world’s most breathtaking landscapes

By Tawqeer | May 8, 2026

This planet of ours is overflowing with natural beauty, filled with landscapes so extraordinary that they often feel almost unreal. From towering mountains wrapped in clouds to crystal-clear lakes, endless deserts, lush forests, and dramatic coastlines, every corner of the Earth holds a masterpiece shaped by nature itself. Across continents and cultures, these breathtaking places remind us just how vast, powerful, and diverse our world truly is.

In this blog, I’ve gathered some of the most stunning landscapes from around the globe, destinations that capture the imagination and leave lasting memories in the hearts of those lucky enough to witness them. Each location tells its own story through its colors, textures, and atmosphere, offering a unique glimpse into the beauty and wonder of our planet. Some inspire a sense of peace and stillness, while others awaken adventure and curiosity.

Whether you’re planning your next unforgettable journey, searching for travel inspiration, or simply looking to escape into the beauty of nature for a few moments, these remarkable places are sure to spark your wanderlust. So sit back, explore these incredible landscapes, and let yourself be transported to some of the most awe-inspiring destinations our world has to offer.

South Africa

Blyde River Canyon

At 25 kilometres long and with an average depth of 750 metres Blyde River Canyon is one of the planet’s largest canyons. Take the Panorama route to see the most breathtaking landscapes of the green clad flanks and the Three Rondavels rock formation. Uniquely recognized as one of the largest "green canyons" in the world, its rich subtropical foliage and diverse wildlife make it a striking contrast to the barren landscapes of typical desert canyons.

Vietnam

Mu Cang Chai

The most spectacular rice terraces in Vietnam corrugate the hillsides of the Mu Cang Chai region, where villages perch among the emerald hillsides and the views down the valley are simply astonishing. This region is not only one of the most photogenic on earth, but also showcases some of Vietnam’s most breathtaking landscapes. These terraces were masterfully carved over centuries by the local Hmong people to cultivate the steep terrain. Make sure you bring your camera, every turn offers a picture-perfect moment.

Bai Tu Long Bay

This is the lesser-known equivalent of the more crowded Halong Bay, offering the same staggering land and seascapes with dramatic eruptions of rock scattered throughout the bay. Because it is further off the typical tourist trail, its waters remain pristine and its floating fishing villages offer a more authentic experience. It is best explored on an overnight cruise so you can enjoy the beautiful karst scenery at sunset and sunrise.

Kyrgyzstan

Song Kol

At a breathtaking altitude of 3,000 meters, staggeringly scenic Song Kol Lake lies in the central Tien Shan range, seducing everyone who passes with its indigo depths and surrounding snow-dusted peaks. Accessible only during the summer months due to heavy mountain snows, this is one of Kyrgyzstan’s most breathtaking landscapes, where nomadic herdsmen often set up yurt camps on the lake shore, adding to the timeless, tranquil beauty of the scene.

Tien Shan Mountains

The idyllic Grigorievskoe Gorge, not far from the shores of Issyk-Kul Lake, is a haven for hikers and horseriders who enjoy the stunning, pristine alpine scenery of the gorge. Translating to the "Mountains of Heaven," this vast system stretches across Central Asia. Discover lush green mountain slopes strewn with flowers and clear rushing streams surrounded by towering peaks.

Japan

Mount Fuji

Enjoy unrivaled views of Mount Fuji when you visit Hakone National Park, a blissful region of undulating trails, mountaintop shrines, and the jewel in the crown – Ashi Lake. As an active stratovolcano and Japan's highest peak, it has been a sacred site of pilgrimage for centuries. Surrounded by forests that blaze with color in autumn, this area is one of Japan’s most breathtaking landscapes. The sight of Mount Fuji reflected in the serene waters of the lake is nothing short of unforgettable.

Blue Pond, Hokkaido

As the water from Mount Tokachi churns down Shirahige Falls, it collects aluminum and other minerals which are said to account for the intense hue of the Blue Pond – the body of water found at the waterfall’s end. Interestingly, the pond is a man-made feature created to protect the town of Biei from volcanic mudflows. Bare, birch tree trunks emerge from the pond giving it an ethereal beauty that shifts in color depending on the weather and season.

Brazil

Iguazu Falls

In the rainy season 13,000 cubic meters of water thunder over almost 3 kilometers of cliffs every second, plunging 80 meters in a spectacular show of nature’s raw power. The most intense section, known as the Devil's Throat (Garganta del Diablo), features a U-shaped chasm where the roar is deafening. Viewing decks allows you to get as close as you dare to the falls, and there are rainforest trails nearby, too.

Guanabara Bay

Best known as the remarkable backdrop for one of the world’s most beautifully sited cities – Rio de Janeiro – Guanabara Bay is an incredible collection of conical mountains and sparkling sea. Discovered by Portuguese explorers in 1502, it is the second-largest bay in Brazil. There can be few landscapes as iconic and jaw dropping as this one, with Sugar Loaf mountain and Corcovado, pedestal of Christ the Redeemer, among the world’s most recognisable.

Indonesia

Gunung Bromo, Java

A cluster of volcanic cones sits inside one huge crater, a nine-kilometer-wide plateau believed to have formed from a massive ancient eruption. Standing in the middle of a vast plain called the "Sea of Sand," it creates a stark and cinematic environment. As part of one of Indonesia’s most breathtaking landscapes, Mount Bromo is a popular destination for watching the sunrise from the crater rim, where the low morning light highlights the surreal detail of this otherworldly terrain.

Raja Ampat

Pristine underwater worlds are the major draw in Raja Ampat’s remote islands, but the scenery above the waves is impressive, too. Translating to "The Four Kings," this archipelago sits at the heart of the Coral Triangle, holding the record for the highest marine biodiversity on Earth. Stunning limestone outcrops rise sheer from the azure water known for its amazing visibility, while paradise beaches fringe the larger islands.

Philippines

Siquijor island

Exquisite Siquijor boasts sleepy villages slung along the shore with green mountains rising behind and perfect aquamarine seascapes in front. Known among locals as an island of magic, healers, and mysticism, it retains an untouched, mysterious charm. If you ever tear yourself away from the mesmerizing beaches, head to Cambugahay Falls for another perfect tropical scene featuring multi-tiered cascading waters.

China

Yangshuo karst formations

The Li River is like a silver ribbon winding its way around and between the stunning karst hills of Yangshuo, and the riverbanks are a checker board of small fields full of crops. These dramatic limestone peaks have inspired Chinese landscape painting for thousands of years. Take a bamboo raft trip to admire the jaw-dropping landscape up close, or visit viewpoints for a birds eye view.

Huangshan mountains

Stunning doesn’t really cover it – the Huangshan Mountains have an ethereal mystery and dramatic geology that makes them seem like they have been lifted from the pages of a fairytale. Famous for its magnificent sunsets, peculiarly shaped granite peaks, and the ancient "Greeting Pine" trees that cling to the cliffs. With numerous needle peaks piercing the swirling mist, it’s easy to see why this is the land of legends, inspiring Chinese artists and poets for many centuries.
